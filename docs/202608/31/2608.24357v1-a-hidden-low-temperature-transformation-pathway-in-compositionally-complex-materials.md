## 摘要
大多数成分复杂材料（CCMs，常被称为高熵合金）是亚稳态的，其吸引人的性能通常属于动力学捕获状态。然而，通向决定长期稳定性的更低自由能相态的路径可能仍然隐藏，因为扩散控制的原子再分布过于缓慢，无法在实验可及的时间尺度上揭示。这一盲区在CCM设计中尤为突出：巨大的成分空间被筛选以追求性能，但在材料选择中很少考虑决定该性能是否持续的低温动力学及相关转变路径。在这里，我们利用富含缺陷的纳米尺度体积结合原子探针层析技术，在不依赖升高温度加速转变的情况下，揭示并重构了一种亚稳态Ag24Au20Pd50Pt6电催化剂中隐藏的相演化路径。通过改变微观结构起始状态、退火温度和时间，我们揭示了面心立方基体内富Pt相的析出、粗化和再均匀化。富Pt相在均匀化后再次出现，但动力学可及性延迟，而延长退火将路径延伸至300°C。原子模拟独立预测了相同的富Pt相选择。该转变伴随着析氢催化活性3.7倍的损失。这些结果确立了隐藏相演化路径作为材料设计变量：解析它们不仅可以指导亚稳态CCM的选择——不仅基于其合成态性能，还基于它们随时间可能达到的相态和相关功能。

## Abstract
Most compositionally complex materials (CCMs, frequently referred to as high entropy alloys) are metastable and their attractive properties often belong to kinetically trapped states. However, pathways towards lower-free-energy phase states governing long-term stability, can remain hidden because diffusion-controlled atomic redistribution is too slow to be revealed at experimentally accessible timescales. This blind spot is acute in CCM design: enormous compositional spaces are screened for performance, yet the low-temperature kinetics and the associated transformation pathways determining whether that performance persists are rarely considered in material selection. Here we use defect-rich nanoscale volumes coupled with atom-probe tomography to access and reconstruct the hidden phase-evolution pathway in a metastable Ag24Au20Pd50Pt6 electrocatalyst, without relying on elevated temperatures to accelerate the transformation. By varying microstructural starting state, annealing temperature and time, we reveal precipitation of a Pt-rich phase within the fcc matrix, its coarsening and re-homogenization. The Pt-rich phase recurs after homogenization with delayed kinetic accessibility, while prolonged annealing extends the pathway to 300°C. Atomistic simulations independently predict the same Pt-rich phase selection. The transformation is accompanied by a 3.7-fold loss of catalytic activity for hydrogen evolution. These results establish hidden phase-evolution pathways as a materials-design variable: resolving them can guide the selection of metastable CCMs not only for their as-synthesized properties, but also for the phase states and associated functionalities they may access over time.
