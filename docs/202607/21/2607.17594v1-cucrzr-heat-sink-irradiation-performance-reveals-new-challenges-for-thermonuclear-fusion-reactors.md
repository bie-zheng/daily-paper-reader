## 摘要
商业聚变能需要材料能够承受强烈的中子轰击，同时提取极端热负荷以转化为电能。CuCrZr合金作为聚变反应堆的主要散热材料，其强度源自时效热处理过程中形成的纳米析出物的精细弥散。这种析出硬化策略能否承受聚变相关辐照尚未得到验证。本文结合重离子辐照和氦注入下的原位透射电子显微镜与热力学和嬗变模型，发现硬化析出物在两种相反的动力学机制下溶解：低温下弹道溶解占主导，高温下溶解和再析出占主导。尽管离子辐照固有的加速剂量率相对于反应堆条件改变了弹道混合与热回扩散之间的平衡，但两种动力学极端下的析出物降解表明，在长期中子暴露下，时效态的微观结构不太可能保持不变。一旦空位变得可移动，氦泡和富Kr空洞就会成核，而五个运行年间的嬗变不可逆地将合金化学组成导向Ni-Zr金属间化合物。这三种独立机制共同挑战了CuCrZr性能所依赖的策略，表明时效硬化型铜基散热合金在聚变反应堆中的长期性能需要进一步评估。我们的发现揭示了聚变反应堆设计和商业化面临的新材料挑战：需要新的铜基散热合金，能够在化学组成被聚变中子谱不可逆地（热力学和弹道地）改写的同时，保持工程强度。

## Abstract
Commercial fusion energy requires materials that survive intense neutron bombardment whilst extracting extreme heat loads for conversion to electricity. The CuCrZr alloy, the leading heat-sink material for fusion reactors, derives its strength from a fine dispersion of nano-precipitates formed during prime-ageing heat-treatment. Whether this precipitation-hardening strategy can withstand fusion-relevant irradiation remains untested. Here we show, combining in situ transmission electron microscopy under heavy-ion irradiation and He implantation with thermodynamic and transmutation modelling, that the hardening precipitates dissolve under two opposing kinetic regimes: ballistic dissolution dominates at low temperatures, whilst dissolution and re-precipitation dominate at high temperatures. Although the accelerated dose rates inherent to ion irradiation shift the balance between ballistic mixing and thermal back-diffusion relative to reactor conditions, precipitate degradation at both kinetic extremes indicates that the prime-aged microstructure is unlikely to remain unaltered under prolonged neutron exposure. He bubbles and Kr-rich voids nucleate once vacancies become mobile, and transmutation over five service years irreversibly redirects the alloy chemistry towards Ni-Zr intermetallics. These three independent mechanisms converge to challenge the strategy on which CuCrZr performance depends, suggesting that the long-term performance of age-hardenable Cu-based heat-sink alloys in fusion reactors warrants further assessment. Our findings reveal a new materials challenge for fusion reactor design and commercialisation: the need for new Cu-based heat-sink alloys able to retain engineered strength whilst their chemistry is irreversibly rewritten - thermodynamically and ballistically - by the fusion neutron spectrum.
